LEARN WITH ME

I have spent years learning things by repeating, correcting and trying again — mostly on the ice. Here are two concrete ways I can help you do the same. Both happen on the rink I already train at, so no extra space, equipment or permission is needed.

01
Practice together

Practice Partner on the Ice

If you already skate but feel stuck on one element, let’s train side by side — and keep each other honest about whether practice is actually paying off.

6–8 week cycle 1–2× / week 60–90 min
Who it’s for
Skaters who know the basics but got stuck on one element — a back outside edge, a three-turn, a first jump — and feel like practising stopped making a difference.
What we’ll do
Skate together once or twice a week, each on our own goal. Every 20 minutes we watch each other once, film a short clip, and name one thing to change. Off the ice we spend 5 minutes writing what improved and what’s next, on one shared log. I won’t teach or contradict your coach — I’m there so someone is training with you, and someone sees your progress.
My experience
I’ve skated for several years. Some elements clicked in a few days; others took months of repeating and correcting before they were stable. I know the feeling of practising hard and seeing nothing change — and how much easier it is to keep going when someone is next to you.
Time I can give
1–2 sessions a week, 60–90 minutes each, folded into my own training. One cycle runs 6–8 weeks.
How to start
Just tell me “I want someone to practise with.” We’ll talk about what you’re working on and where you’re stuck, then set one 6-week goal — for example, making the back outside edge feel stable.
02
Company & encouragement

Your First Time on the Ice

Never skated — or came once, fell hard and didn’t want to go back? I’ll stay next to you for the first half hour, until the ice stops feeling scary.

About 2 hours Max 2× / month 4–6 times a term
Who it’s for
Anyone who has never been on the ice, or came once, fell hard and got scared — especially younger students at their first skating event.
What we’ll do
Ten minutes on the ground first: how to fall, how to stand up, the rules of the rink. Then 30 minutes right beside you, holding on until you can walk on your own. I won’t teach technique — the point is that the ice feels safe and that falling is survivable. We finish with one small win, like letting go of the boards for a few metres.
My experience
The first time I stepped on the ice I wouldn’t let go of the boards, and I fell hard enough to remember it. Someone stayed next to me while I let go step by step — that’s how I learned that fear can be walked through when you’re not alone. I’ve since taken friends onto the ice for the first time.
Time I can give
About 2 hours per session, at most twice a month — roughly 4–6 sessions in a term.
How to start
Tell me a week before an open session or a class event and I’ll confirm the time, or simply ask “can you come on the ice with me this Saturday?”
Ask me about

THINGS I CAN TALK YOU THROUGH

How progress actually happens
Why some things improve in days and others take months — and how to tell the difference between “not working” and “not finished”.
Getting unstuck on one element
How I break a stuck skill into smaller pieces, film it, and pick the single change worth making that week.
Keeping going when it feels pointless
What I do on the days practice feels like a waste — the log I keep, and how I decide something is worth one more week.
